## How Do I Write Review Request Language That Produces Detailed, Keyword-Rich Reviews ChatGPT Is More Likely to Surface?

Reviews on Google Business Profile, Trustpilot, and Yelp are the most powerful brand data signals a window replacement company controls directly - and the specific language inside those reviews determines whether an AI engine surfaces them in response to a homeowner's prompt. Generic five-star reviews with no detail give OpenAI nothing to cite. Detailed reviews that name the window type, the installation experience, and the outcome give the AI exactly the structured brand data it needs.

Use this review request formula with every completed window replacement job:

> "We'd love a quick review! If you have 60 seconds, mention: (1) what type of windows we installed, (2) how the installation went, and (3) what difference you see in your home now. Specific details help other homeowners find the right window contractor."

This prompt structure works because it guides customers to answer the same questions a homeowner types into ChatGPT when they ask for the best window replacement companies in their area. When OpenAI's AI engine reads a review that says "double-hung vinyl windows, installed in one day, no drafts since" - that review answers a real search prompt with real brand data.

**Three rules for review language that AI engines frequently surface:**

- **Name the product** - "casement windows," "triple-pane glass," or "energy-efficient replacement windows" beats "great work."
- **Name the outcome** - "energy bill dropped $40 a month" is a concrete, citable fact; "very happy" is not.
- **Name the location** - City and neighborhood data in a review reinforces the Google Business Profile geo-signal that tells OpenAI your brand serves that specific market for window replacement.

A window replacement company that collects 20 detailed reviews using this formula builds stronger AI brand signals in 30 days than a competitor with 200 generic reviews built over two years.

## How to Audit Which Competitors ChatGPT Recommends Using AI Overview Data and Reverse-Engineer Their Shared Signals

Run this audit now: open ChatGPT and enter the prompt "Who are the best window replacement companies in [your city]?" - then record every business name the engine returns.

Next, run the same prompt in Google to see which businesses appear in the **AI Overview** panel. Cross-reference both lists. The businesses that appear in both ChatGPT and Google AI Overview share a specific cluster of signals - and that cluster is what you reverse-engineer.

**Here is what to look for when you audit each competitor:**

1. **Review volume and recency** - Count total reviews on Google Business Profile, Yelp, and Trustpilot. Businesses ChatGPT cites frequently carry 150+ reviews with responses posted within 48 hours of each review.
2. **NAP consistency** - Check that the business name, address, and phone number match exactly on every listing. Inconsistent data breaks the entity signal AI engines use to confirm a brand is real and local.
3. **Review content depth** - Read the actual review text. Reviews that mention specific window types (double-hung, casement, bay window) give AI engines richer data to match the business to specific replacement queries.
4. **Third-party citation footprint** - Search the competitor brand name and see how many authoritative directories, local news sites, and home-service platforms cite them. A dense citation network tells OpenAI's engine the brand is established.
5. **Google Business Profile completeness** - Check their service list, photo count, and Q&A section. Gaps in your own profile that competitors have filled are the fastest wins to close.

Only 45% of brands performing well in traditional Google rankings also appear in AI recommendations.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-3" id="ref-cit_003-2">[3]</a></sup> That gap is the non-obvious insight most window contractors miss: **strong Google SEO does not automatically produce AI visibility** - the two engines weight different signals, and a brand must optimize for both independently.

The hidden trade-off here is review platform distribution. Contractors who concentrate all reviews on Google Business Profile score well in traditional search but get passed over by ChatGPT, which pulls from a broader brand entity graph that includes Trustpilot, Yelp, and third-party home-service directories. Spread your review generation effort for window replacement services across all three platforms - not just Google - and your brand entity becomes legible to OpenAI's data sources in a way a Google-only strategy never achieves.

## How to Prompt-Test Your Own ChatGPT Visibility, Document Results, and Track Data Over Time

Run a structured prompt-test audit of your window replacement company's ChatGPT visibility right now - because you cannot fix what you have not measured, and most window contractors have never checked whether they appear at all.

**The hidden trade-off most competitors miss:** AI engines like ChatGPT produce inconsistent answers. Research involving 600 volunteers who ran 12 different prompts through AI tools a combined 2,961 times found that AI systems are highly inconsistent when recommending brands or products.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-5" id="ref-cit_007-1">[5]</a></sup> This means a single test is not enough - you need a repeatable, documented testing cadence to see real patterns in your visibility data.

Here is the exact process to run your prompt-test audit:

1. **Write 5 to 8 test prompts** that mirror how real homeowners ask ChatGPT for window replacement help. Examples: "Who are the best window replacement companies in [your city]?" and "Which window replacement contractors do you recommend for window replacement in [your metro area]?"
2. **Run each prompt in a fresh ChatGPT session** - a new conversation window with no prior context - so prior chat history does not skew the answer.
3. **Record the full response verbatim** in a tracking spreadsheet. Log the date, the prompt text, whether your brand name appeared, where it appeared in the answer (first mention, third mention, not at all), and what language the AI used to describe your business.
4. **Repeat the same prompts weekly** for at least four weeks before drawing conclusions. OpenAI's models do not update in real time, so short-term data produces false negatives.
5. **Flag the exact phrasing ChatGPT uses** when it does cite your company. That language frequently mirrors your Google Business Profile description, your review text, or your answer-block page content - which tells you which asset is driving the citation.

The non-obvious insight here: OpenAI's entity extraction is inconsistent even for the same brand name across sessions.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-6" id="ref-cit_005-1">[6]</a></sup> A window replacement company that appears in 3 out of 8 prompts is not failing - it is at an early stage of brand entity recognition. The goal of your SEO and review-building work is to push that appearance rate above 6 out of 8 prompts consistently. **Track the ratio, not just the presence.** That ratio is the most reliable data point you have for measuring AI visibility progress for window replacement businesses over time.
