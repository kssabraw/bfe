## How to Run a Monthly AI Visibility Audit Using Prompt Testing Across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini

**A monthly AI visibility audit tells you exactly where your HVAC company appears - and where it does not - across every major AI search engine that homeowners use to find contractors.** Most HVAC operators assume they rank well because their Google Maps pin shows up. That assumption is wrong. AI search and traditional search engine results are diverging fast, and a business that ranks on page one of Google does not automatically get recommended by ChatGPT or Perplexity.

Here is the non-obvious trade-off most competitors miss: **AI Overview content changes 70% of the time for the same query, with nearly half of citations replaced on each regeneration.**<sup><a href="#sources-cited-5" id="ref-cit_006-4">[5]</a></sup> That means a single audit snapshot is nearly worthless. Monthly testing across multiple platforms is the only way to detect whether your HVAC company holds a consistent citation presence or only appears intermittently.

**Run your monthly audit in four steps:**

1. **Build a prompt set of 8-12 queries** that mirror how real homeowners ask AI assistants for help - for example: "Who are the top-rated HVAC companies in [your city]?" and "Which HVAC company should I call for same-day AC repair in [your service area]?" Vary phrasing across prompts; do not rely on a single query.
2. **Test each prompt across ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini** on the same day each month. Screenshot or log every response. Record which businesses are named, how they are described, and which sources are cited.
3. **Track citation sources, not just mentions.** When your HVAC business appears, identify the exact URL or directory the AI cited. That tells you which data sources are feeding the recommendation - Google Business Profile, Yelp, Angi, or a local editorial list.
4. **Log changes month over month** in a simple spreadsheet: prompt text, platform, your HVAC company mentioned (yes/no), citation source, and competitor names that appeared instead.

The insider perspective most SEO guides skip: **AI platforms crawl your content to generate answers without sending traffic back**, meaning AI-driven searches show extreme zero-click behavior at approximately 93% compared to approximately 34% in traditional search.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-5" id="ref-cit_006-3">[5]</a></sup> Your HVAC company can be recommended dozens of times per day and never see a referral visit in Google Analytics. Audit tools that track only web traffic miss this entirely.

## The NAP Consistency Failure Modes That Confuse AI Citation Systems - And a Field Audit Checklist to Fix Them

NAP inconsistency is the single most common reason a well-listed HVAC company fails to get recommended by ChatGPT - and most owners do not know it is happening until a competitor shows up in AI answers instead.

NAP stands for Name, Address, and Phone number, which is the core business identity data that should match everywhere your business appears online.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-6" id="ref-cit_007-4">[6]</a></sup> When AI systems and search engine crawlers encounter conflicting versions of your business data across directories, they cannot confidently consolidate your authority into a single entity. The result: your HVAC company gets fragmented across multiple unresolved records, and AI systems skip you in favor of a competitor whose data is clean.

**The non-obvious trade-off most SEO guides miss:** even small variations like abbreviations or suite formatting - "Ste 4" versus "Suite 4" versus "#4" - create multiple versions of the same business online.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-6" id="ref-cit_007-3">[6]</a></sup> AI citation systems treat these as separate entities. Inconsistent NAP data in citations dilutes your local SEO efforts because search engines cannot consolidate the authority of those citations, which weakens their impact on your local search rankings.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-3" id="ref-cit_002-3">[3]</a></sup> Consistent NAP information, by contrast, strengthens those signals and improves your business's chances of ranking in the Local Pack and Google Maps.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-3" id="ref-cit_002-2">[3]</a></sup>

NAP inconsistencies most often come from rebrands, address changes, phone number updates, duplicate listings, and directory data aggregation that spreads old or incorrect information.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-6" id="ref-cit_007-2">[6]</a></sup> The fix is a structured audit - run it quarterly and immediately after any change to your business name, address, or phone number.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-6" id="ref-cit_007-1">[6]</a></sup>

**HVAC NAP Audit Checklist - Run This Before Any New Directory Submission:**

1. **Set your canonical NAP record.** Write out the exact legal business name, full street address (including suite format you will use everywhere), and primary phone number. This is your source of truth.
2. **Search Google for your business name + city.** Every listing that surfaces is a potential inconsistency. Log each one.
3. **Check the top six directories directly.** Pull your live listings on Google Business Profile, Yelp, Angi, Thumbtack, BBB, and Trustpilot. Compare each field character-by-character against your canonical record.
4. **Search for old phone numbers and addresses.** If your business moved or changed numbers in the last five years, search those old details to find stale listings still pointing to outdated data.
5. **Identify duplicate listings and request removal.** Duplicate listings on the same platform split your review equity and confuse AI citation systems. Flag and remove them.
6. **Correct abbreviation mismatches.** Standardize "Street" vs. "St.", "Suite" vs. "Ste.", and business name suffixes like "LLC" or "Inc." across every listing.
7. **Document every correction with a timestamp.** AI systems re-crawl directories on irregular schedules. Tracking your fix dates helps you correlate when AI recommendations improve.

**The fastest way to audit at scale** is to run your business through a structured off-page presence tool that surfaces every directory mention and flags mismatches automatically - rather than checking each platform manually. Tools like the Off-Page Agent surface these gaps in minutes, so you spend time fixing records instead of hunting for them.

## Use Schema Markup and SEO to Structure Service Pages So ChatGPT Can Cite Your HVAC Company

Add structured data and semantic depth to every HVAC service page so AI systems have clean, citable information to pull from when a user asks for a recommendation. Without this, your pages exist in a search engine but remain invisible to the retrieval logic that drives AI recommendations.

Here is the problem most HVAC companies face: their service pages are built for humans to skim, not for AI to extract and cite. A page that says "We fix AC units" gives ChatGPT nothing concrete to quote. A page that explains what the service covers, which equipment brands are supported, what the response time is, and where the service area sits - that page gives an AI system something to work with.

**Schema markup alone does not guarantee AI citations.** Domains with complete schema coverage perform no better than domains with minimal or no schema across OpenAI, Gemini, and Perplexity.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-4" id="ref-cit_003-3">[4]</a></sup> Visibility inside AI search depends on semantic relevance and model retrieval behavior rather than structured markup.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-4" id="ref-cit_003-2">[4]</a></sup> That means your real leverage is in the content itself - not just the technical wrapper around it.
