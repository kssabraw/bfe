## How to Structure Project Case Study Pages So AI Search Engines Cite Your Deck Build Company as a Local Authority

Structure every deck build case study page around four concrete sections: the homeowner's problem, the materials and build specs chosen, the local code or HOA constraint navigated, and the measurable outcome (square footage, timeline, cost range). This is the non-obvious angle most deck builder SEO guides skip: AI search engines like ChatGPT pull citations from pages that answer a complete question in one place, not pages that tease details and redirect.

Here is the insider trade-off competitors rarely mention - a vague case study that says "we built a beautiful composite deck" earns zero citations from AI systems, while a page that states "this 400-square-foot Trex Transcend deck in [City Name] required a setback variance under [County] code and was completed in 11 business days" becomes a citable fact source.

For deck builders serious about digital market dominance, structure case study pages using this framework:

| Section | What to Include | Why It Drives AI Citation |
|---|---|---|
| Project Scope | Square footage, material brand, deck type | Answers dimensional queries directly |
| Local Constraint | Permit office, HOA rule, setback distance | Signals hyper-local authority to search engines |
| Build Timeline | Start date, completion date, weather factor | Gives AI a verifiable, specific data point |
| Cost Range | Budget tier (e.g., $18K–$24K), not exact | Answers the #1 homeowner search query |

Schema markup on these pages helps AI platforms understand your deck build content as structured data.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-5" id="ref-cit_006-2">[5]</a></sup> Adding LocalBusiness and Review schema to each case study page reinforces your website's authority signal across Google and LLM-based search engines.<sup><a href="#sources-cited-5" id="ref-cit_006-1">[5]</a></sup>
